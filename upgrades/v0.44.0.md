## What changed

- **`scripts/delegation-guard.sh`** (new): a `PreToolUse` hook on
  `mcp__.*__create_session|Agent|Task`. Returns `ask` — never `deny` — when
  opening delegated work leaves something undecided, across three families:

  - **TIER** — no `model` passed, or the child's prompt never names its effort.
    Without `model` the child silently inherits the caller's. The ask names the
    ladder by **reading `scripts/route-tiers.txt`** rather than repeating it, so
    retuning the ladder retunes the message.
  - **CONTEXT** — the prompt carries no anchor (an issue, a URL, a path), or is
    over 8000 characters, which is a pasted contract rather than a pointer to
    one. The reason recites the four parts of a right-sized context — anchor,
    delta, collisions, traps — every time it fires, including when it fired for
    another family: if you are going to stop someone, the stop should serve all
    three.
  - **FANOUT** — an issue this session already delegated (a duplicate, or a
    relaunch that should first check what the previous child left behind), or
    the fourth child onwards, where N sessions drain the same quota N times
    faster.

- **`scripts/delegation-record.sh`** (new): the writing half, on `PostToolUse`.
  It sits there and not on `PreToolUse` deliberately — in Pre the child may
  still never exist (the user denies, the call fails), and a counter of
  intentions would accuse a child that never happened of being a duplicate. The
  counter has to count facts. State is one JSONL line per child in the system
  temp dir keyed by parent session: **not** under the project, where it would
  dirty `git status` and end up committed. Ephemeral by design — losing it only
  means the guard stops warning, never that it blocks.

Both fail open: no `python3`, unreadable JSON or any other tool and they print
nothing and exit 0.

## Why

The plan's `Route` column has always routed each task to a tier, and
`flywheel-work` §*Honor the plan's route* tells a session to switch **up** when a
task sits above its tier. Nothing was written for the other direction, and
nothing looked at the moment a tier is actually chosen: when a session is
created.

On 2026-09-13 a coordinator running on opus fanned out five jobs. Three of them
(streams A, B and E) had no opus task in the plan at all and the other two had
opus in the minority — and all five inherited opus because `model` was never
passed. It cost roughly $25 more than the routed tiers, and nothing surfaced it:
a silent default leaves no trace. The same session then nearly relaunched a job
that had already finished and opened its PR, which a duplicate check would have
caught.

The three families are the three ways that delegation goes wrong, and only the
first is visible afterwards. The wrong tier is paid in the bill; the wrong
context is paid in a child burning its window rediscovering what was already
known — which shows up in no metric, it just looks slow; and the child that
should not exist is paid in both.

## What it does not do

It decides nothing. Inheriting a model, pasting a contract and fanning out wide
are all legitimate **when deliberate** — a job whose riskiest step is
`opus/high` wants opus, and a subagent with no repo access needs the text. A
`deny` would have to be worked around, and a guard that gets worked around stops
guarding. What is prevented is doing any of it without having decided.

## Action required

None. Re-vendor (`/flywheel:update`) to pick the hooks up; they register
themselves through `hooks/hooks.json`. Nothing in an installed repo changes, no
skill behaviour changed, and the guard is silent on every call that already
passes `model` and names its effort.
