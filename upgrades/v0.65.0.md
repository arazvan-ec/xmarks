## What changed

The cost roll-up that v0.64.0 made possible said the route ladder had collapsed:
of 46 routed transitions, **40 ran on opus**, sonnet 5, **haiku 0**, and
`+delegate` appeared once in 58 lines. The obvious reading — the planner
over-assigns, or `work` disobeys — is **wrong**. Measured per task, every
transition that maps 1:1 to a plan task honored its route, 5 of 5.

The ladder evaporates three other ways, none of which any gate asked about:

- **The cheap tail is never recorded.** Eight planned tasks across p13, p42 and
  p43 have no transition line at all — **five of them the `haiku/low+delegate`
  ones**. The ledger cannot tell *"ran and wrote nothing"* from *"never ran"*,
  which is the finding rather than a detail of it.
- **A merge silently buys the higher tier.** p43's one `T1-T2` transition runs at
  the max of the two, so T1's cheaper route was bought and never used.
- **A route can be unrankable.** 26 lines carry `opus/xhigh`; `route-tiers.txt`
  defines `low|medium|high|max`. A route nothing can rank is compared against
  nothing and passes as if honored.

`scripts/check-route-honored.sh` compares the two, per task. It **fails** on the
cases the record can settle — a plan task with no line, and a transition that ran
above its plan's tier with no `route_escalated_from` — and only from the same
cutoff v0.64.0 introduced, so the existing corpus is a counted debt rather than a
red tree. It **reports** what it cannot settle: merges, unrankable routes, and
transitions that map to no plan task. Absence is always reported as absence.

`plan-route.sh` gained `--json` so the plan format keeps one parser; the ladders
travel with the parse so nothing ranks a route against a second copy of them.

`skills/work` gains the half of its rule the evidence exposed: it said *never
downgrade silently*, while the observed drift is **upward**, and through merges.

Nothing to do on upgrade. If your repo runs cycles without a plan, they are
counted and skipped — there is no route to honor.
